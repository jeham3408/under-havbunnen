Methods and sources

Our hackathon project followed changes in published Norwegian field estimates and checked early UK estimates against production. This separate, hypothetical example lets you see how an assumption changes a volumetric estimate.

Recoverable stock-tank oil = area × gross thickness × net-to-gross ratio × porosity × oil saturation ÷ oil formation volume factor × recovery factor.

The fixed assumptions are a 20 km² mapped area, 65% net reservoir rock, 75% initial oil saturation and a formation volume factor of 1.25 reservoir m³ per standard m³. One standard cubic metre of oil is 6.2898 barrels. The coloured layer has exaggerated height.

The calculation uses one average value per property across the field. Recovery is an assumption. Estimating it for a real field requires information about fluid flow, wells and production history. Each slider setting gives one illustrative result. The bars share a linear scale from zero to 500 million m³, with the reference conditions labelled above each group.
