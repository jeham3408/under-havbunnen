Methods and sources

The model estimates how much oil could be recovered from a hypothetical field. Porosity is the fraction of the rock volume made up of pores. Oil saturation is the fraction of that pore space filled with oil. The recovery factor sets how much of that oil can be produced.

Area × gross thickness × net-to-gross ratio × porosity × oil saturation × recovery factor ÷ formation volume factor

We fix the area at 20 km², net-to-gross ratio at 65%, oil saturation at 75%, and oil formation volume factor at 1.25 reservoir m³ per standard m³. One standard cubic metre of oil is 6.2898 barrels. The gold layer marks the reservoir. Each combination of assumptions gives one possible outcome.

The curve adds up reported oil production through the selected year, using data from the UK North Sea Transition Authority. The dashed line is an early estimate of how much oil the field could produce over its lifetime.

The sample covers 35 UK oil fields that have stopped producing and have both an early estimate and production records covering their operating lives. Watkins supplied 26 estimates and the DTI Brown Book supplied nine. Each field retains the units of its original estimate. The 2026 records run through August.
