Equation, measurements and limits

The 24 points are approximate readings digitised from figure 10b. The line is the authors’ fit to this experiment. Day zero is the start of the pressure hold.

k(t) = 38 + 480 / (1 + t / 20.4)

Here t is in days and k in µD. For the same fluid viscosity and pressure gradient, Darcy’s law gives q(t) / q(0) = k(t) / k(0). The chart gives a relative flux under those assumptions. A well leak rate needs its flow path, pressure gradient and fluid properties.

Pressure calculation and limits

This vertical interval uses true vertical depth below the rig datum. The atlas estimates reservoir pressure at 1,795.9 m from an equivalent density of 1.179 g/cm³, after subtracting 0.050 from the recorded mud weight of 1.229 g/cm³. The assumed gas density is 0.200 g/cm³.

Prequired = ρreservoir-equivalent g zreservoir − ρgas g (zreservoir − z)

Pproxy = ρtest-equivalent g z

Both tests at 954 and 1,774 m gave an equivalent density of 1.770 g/cm³. Using kg/m³, metres and g = 9.80665 m/s² gives pressure in pascals. A positive margin meets this pressure criterion alone. Pipe contact and hydraulic sealing require separate well evidence.
